Context

Connexions work sits at the intersection of enterprise process, adoption, and service quality.

Connexions Data Inc. supports SAP consulting, infrastructure, staffing, and digital transformation work. That meant the design challenge was not just visual cleanup. The experience had to clarify business rules, support implementation teams, and reduce the support burden that comes from confusing enterprise workflows.

I framed both initiatives around adoption: users needed to understand where they were in a process, what information was required, what errors meant, and how to recover without depending on support.

NDA-safe framing

Real process, recreated visuals.

Production screens, client names, internal workflows, proprietary data, and business logic are removed. The visuals use dummy data and generalized workflows, while the design process, constraints, stakeholder needs, and impact metrics reflect the actual work.

Shared process

A repeatable design process for complex enterprise systems.

Both projects followed the same progression: understand the business workflow, align stakeholders around friction, prototype safer paths, then validate whether the new patterns improved completion and accessibility.

Project 01 | Work in progress

Reducing ERP onboarding abandonment by simplifying first-time setup.

The onboarding flow asked users to complete important setup tasks before they had enough context. I redesigned the experience around role selection, progressive disclosure, inline validation, and a final review moment.

Discovery themes

Friction clustered around confidence and timing.

  • Progress was not visible enough for a multi-step enterprise task.
  • ERP terms appeared before users understood why they mattered.
  • Validation happened too late, making recovery feel expensive.
  • Different user roles needed different setup paths.

Iterations and brainstorming

Three directions tested before converging on a guided setup model.

Early concepts explored whether to compress the flow, split it into wizard steps, or create a readiness checklist. The selected direction combined a stepper with a checklist-style review so users could see both progress and missing requirements.

Iteration A

Single-page setup

Fast for expert admins, but too dense for first-time users.

Iteration B

Wizard flow

Clearer progression, but needed stronger recovery and review.

Selected

Guided setup + review

Balanced progress, validation, and enterprise confidence.

Discarded concept A

Single-page setup

Why it was discarded: it looked efficient, but compressed too many ERP decisions into one page and increased cognitive load for new users.

Discarded concept B

Linear wizard only

Why it was discarded: it made progress visible, but users could not easily understand what was missing before submission.

Chosen direction

Guided setup + review

Why it won: it preserved required business logic while adding progress, inline validation, and a review checkpoint.

ERP reflection

What I learned

Enterprise onboarding could not be simplified by removing required logic. The stronger move was sequencing decisions, explaining terms earlier, and giving users a review checkpoint before commitment.

ERP next steps

Where I would improve next

Expand usability testing by role, validate save-and-resume behavior, and measure whether clearer setup requirements reduce support tickets after launch.

Accessibility audit

Findings were prioritized by task completion risk.

Critical Keyboard users could lose track of focus in ticket actions.
High Form errors were not specific enough to recover quickly.
Medium Case status relied too heavily on color and placement.
Low Spacing and label consistency varied across portal screens.
Stakeholder alignment

Accessibility decisions connected user need to service outcomes.

  • Support teams needed fewer avoidable ticket clarifications.
  • Engineering needed component-level states and focus specs.
  • Client stakeholders needed confidence around WCAG 2.1 AA alignment.
  • Users needed predictable form, status, and navigation behavior.

Accessibility reflection

What I learned

Accessibility had the most impact when it was treated as a full journey issue. Contrast mattered, but keyboard order, errors, status language, and semantic structure mattered just as much.

Accessibility next steps

Where I would improve next

Run screen-reader walkthroughs with representative users, document component-level accessibility rules, and continue tracking engagement by task type.

Reflection

Enterprise UX is about sequencing complexity, not pretending complexity does not exist.

Across both projects, the strongest design decisions came from making required business logic easier to understand, recover from, and validate. The ERP work reduced onboarding friction; the portal work made accessibility visible across the full service journey.
