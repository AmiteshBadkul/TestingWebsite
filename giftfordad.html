Context

What if a gift could keep creating value after it was opened?

The project began with a personal gift for my father, but I approached it as a product and service design problem. Instead of designing around the object alone, I looked at the full relationship around the gift: the sender, the recipient, the co-gifter, family routines, distance, planning habits, and the need for visible documentation.

The final direction became The Crazy Planner, a relationship-centered planning space that connects emotional intent with practical everyday use. It starts like a gift, then becomes a shared system for planning, printing, displaying, and celebrating progress.

Reflection

The project moved from object-based gifting to experience-system design.

The strongest design decision was treating the gift as a connected service: emotional reveal, family participation, daily usefulness, physical documentation, and celebration over time.

What worked

Specific recipient research kept the concept grounded and made each feature easier to justify.

What needs validation

Long-term engagement, print usage, family participation, and whether support ever feels like pressure.

What I would improve next

Test the flow with more sender-recipient pairs, simplify onboarding, and prototype printable exports in more detail.
